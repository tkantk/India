# 160 landmarks, for you to approve or swap

**32 places, five landmarks each.** Every tile name was measured against the two
budgets the schema enforces and all 160 pass. Every photograph named below was
opened and looked at — the rule being that a landmark whose picture does not
show the landmark ships an empty promise, which is how Konark got a photograph
without its wheel.

Spread across the whole country: 45 built · 34 wild · 34 water · 25 people · 22 high.

**How to use this.** Skim the first section — it is the things I am least sure
about, and it is where your judgement is worth most. Then skim the regions. You
are looking for two answers only: *"not that one"* and *"that one instead"*.
Everything else I can take from here.

### 3. Judgement calls, not evidence. Overrule any of these freely.

- **Puducherry's fifth is a petrified tree stump** in the botanical garden. The *idea* is wonderful for a six-year-old — a tree that turned to stone. The *photograph* is hazy and the stump is small in the frame. This is the weakest tile I am proposing anywhere. Alternative: Aayi Mandapam (`File:Aayi Mandapam in Bharathi Park, Puducherry.jpg`) — clean, bright, no people, and dull.
- **Diu's fifth is limpets on a rock.** I disagree with the reviewer who loved it — I opened it and it is grey and subtle, and reads as "a rock" at tile size. I am using it only because cutting the Vasona lion is not optional (a `©Paül14` watermark that cannot be cropped, on a *captive* animal duplicating Gir). If you'll accept the watermark, the lion is the better picture — your call, and say so in the line that the forest is fenced.
- **Chhattisgarh's Dokra brasses.** The Commons description text is a generic blurb about *West Bengal and Odisha* metalsmiths; only the file title and the categories ("Dokra in Chhattisgarh", "Art of Chhattisgarh") place the objects in Bastar. Medium confidence on provenance. I chose file **03**, not the reviewer's 01 — 01 has three big white "1500/-" price stickers across the front and a stallholder's torso in it; 03 is brass deer and a receding row of brass elephants, no stickers, no people.
- **I kept all four hilltop monasteries** (Key, Thiksey, Rumtek, Tawang) against the child-experience reviewer's advice. Rumtek's photo is a wet courtyard and Key's is a snowbound valley — only Thiksey and Tawang genuinely repeat, and they are two states apart. Each is its state's single best built thing; swapping one out is a net loss. The offered replacement, Hidimba Devi, has **five or six people in frame, several identifiable**, which is the rule that already excluded Cheraw and the Hornbill Festival.
- **Tamil Nadu now has no animal.** I took the reviewer's Mudumalai→Pichavaram swap because Mudumalai and Bandipur are literally the same forest with the same mother-and-calf picture in adjacent states. Mudumalai is the named swap-back if you want the elephant.
- **Assam's Majuli tile is now the masks, not the island.** The old photo was a river at dawn under a sentence about people farming an island — the Konark failure. Rather than write down to the picture, I moved the tile.

### 4. One tile name I had to invent

**"Ranganathittu" is 13 characters and fails the word budget** — the reviewers both passed it, and the schema comment is explicit that 13 is not safe. The tile reads **"Bird Islands"**, which is what the place actually is: six islets in the Kaveri, covered in nesting birds, reached by boat. And **"Kanchenjunga" sits at exactly 12** with zero headroom, as previously flagged.
